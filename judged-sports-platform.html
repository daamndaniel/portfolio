01 · Overview

Fast sport, nuanced judgment

Judged sports rely on deep human expertise. But performances move fast, criteria are nuanced, and the path from performance to decision is not always easy to understand.

XGrab explores how AI can make performance signals more visible, consistent, and useful while keeping human judgment at the center of the experience.

Rather than designing a generic sports platform, we are starting with the complexity of moguls, aerials, and water ramps. These disciplines are our proving ground for building a product that can eventually support a wider world of judged competition.

03 · The opportunity

How might we use AI to support more understandable, scalable judging experiences, without losing the nuance that makes judged sports special?

The challenge is bigger than creating an interface. It is about building trust. Athletes, coaches, judges, organizers, and fans need to understand what the platform is showing, why it matters, and where human expertise remains essential.

That means the brand and product experience have to feel both progressive and credible: technically capable, but never detached from the sport itself.

04 · Design principles
01

Make complex performance easier to understand

The platform should help people make sense of high-speed, highly technical performance without oversimplifying it. Every interaction is an opportunity to turn dense information into something more understandable, actionable, and meaningful.

02

Design trust from the beginning

In a space where decisions matter, clarity is not a nice-to-have. The experience must communicate what the platform knows, how it supports decision-making, and where human judgment still leads. Trust should be built into the system, not added as an afterthought.

03

Build deep before building wide

Starting with moguls, aerials, and water ramps lets us go deep into real sport-specific complexity before scaling further. The design system needs to support that depth today while staying flexible for future disciplines, users, and capabilities.

Identity built from motion, not a static mark

The brand needed to live at the intersection of sport, technology, and human performance.

I am shaping a visual identity that balances the energy of action sports with the credibility required for a platform connected to judging and analysis: modern and ambitious without becoming cold, overly technical, or disconnected from the athletes at its center.

The visual language is designed to support movement, data, confidence, and momentum; it gives the platform a distinct point of view from the first interaction.

Foundations first: tokens, then components

A fast-moving early-stage product needs more than polished screens. It needs a design foundation that helps the team make better decisions, stay aligned, and avoid rebuilding the same patterns over and over.

Foundational tokens for color, type, spacing, and layout
Variables that let the product scale with consistency
Reusable components with purposeful variants
Clear patterns for states, interaction, and responsive behavior
Documentation that makes the system easier to use

06 · Early signal

Too early for metrics

This project is still in progress, so it is too early to measure product impact through adoption or performance metrics. However, the platform has already generated interest from several potential investors.

That interest is not product-market fit, but it is an encouraging early signal that the problem, vision, and opportunity are resonating.

For now, success means building a credible foundation: a clear brand, a scalable system, and a product direction strong enough to invite the right conversations.

XGrab is an ongoing experiment in building that kind of future: one where sport, technology, systems thinking, and human judgment move forward together.
