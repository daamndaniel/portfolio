01 · Overview

A clearer path to sign-up

I redesigned a high-traffic sales landing page to create a clearer, more confidence-building path to sign-up.

The project focused on a simple idea: every field, message, and call to action should help users understand what they are doing, why it matters, and what happens next. By reducing friction at key decision points, the redesign turned more interest into meaningful action.

02 · The challenge

Traffic arrived. Confidence didn't.

AEffort asked before trust was earned
BUnclear what happens after submitting
CHesitation redirected into support requests

The original page brought in traffic but lost too many users before they completed the sign-up process.

Visitors faced a form experience that required effort before it had clearly earned their confidence. Frequent “Get Help” clicks also revealed that uncertainty was interrupting the journey and creating avoidable support demand.

The opportunity was not to make the page look better: it was to make the next step feel worth taking.

03 · My role

Research, then redesign

I approached the redesign through a CRO lens, using user feedback and behavioral analytics to identify where users hesitated or abandoned the experience.

I translated those insights into a more focused form journey, clearer content hierarchy, and more intentional support moments, balancing business conversion goals with a more human user experience.

The decision path
Design question

How might we turn a high-traffic landing page into a low-friction, confidence-building path to sign-up?

Rather than treating conversion as one final click, I looked at the entire journey as a series of small decisions. That revealed three high-impact opportunities.

Three moves that changed the journey

04 · Approach
Click a move or use ↑ ↓
Fields grouped, so the effort stays visible and finite

I simplified the form structure and strengthened the information hierarchy so users could quickly understand what was being asked, why it mattered, and where they were headed.

The goal was to reduce perceived effort. A form should feel like progress, not a barrier.

The value is stated before the ask

I refined the primary CTA and the content surrounding it to better communicate the value of taking the next step.

The CTA became more than a submission button. It became a clear commitment point that helped users understand the benefit of continuing.

Help arrives at the moment of doubt, not beside the main action

Support should be available before users feel lost.

I made help more visible and intentional around likely moments of hesitation, helping users resolve uncertainty without abandoning the flow or losing momentum.

A redesigned conversion journey

This was more than a visual refresh. It was a conversion journey rebuilt around clarity, confidence, and momentum.

By reducing friction and making each decision point easier to understand, the experience helped more users move from curiosity to action.

06 · What I learned

Conversion design is trust design.

When people understand what is being asked of them, why it matters, and what happens next, completing a form feels less like work and more like progress.
