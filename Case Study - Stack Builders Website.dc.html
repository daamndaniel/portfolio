01 · Overview

A site that works as a business tool

I supported the start of the Stack Builders website redesign, then led the update itself. The site is now in implementation. I act as mentor and reviewer for the new sections and components as they get built.

Across those phases the work moved from research and wireframes into prototyping, visual design, and testing. Nothing is live yet: the case study covers the decisions made and the standard the build is being held to.

Research Wireframes Prototypes Visual design Testing Mentorship
02 · The challenge

It worked. It just didn't persuade.

AA visual language that felt outdated
BNavigation that made exploring harder
CA value proposition that arrived too late

The previous website worked, but it was not doing enough to persuade.

Its visual language felt outdated, the navigation made exploration harder than it needed to be, and the company's value proposition was not clear early enough. Visitors had to work too hard to understand what Stack Builders offered, who it could help, and where to go next.

That confusion weakened engagement, credibility, and conversion.

03 · My role

Support, lead, then mentor

I came in at the start of the redesign in a supporting role, contributing to the research, the audit of the existing site, and the first structural explorations that set its direction.

Once that direction held, I led the update: owning the user experience, the visual language, and the messaging changes, and validating them through prototypes and testing. Today my role is narrower and quieter. I mentor the designers carrying the work forward, and I review each new section and component against the system we set, so the site stays coherent as it is built.

Design question

How might we turn an outdated website into a clear, credible experience that helps more visitors understand the value of Stack Builders and act on it?

The answer came down to three connected design decisions.

04 · Approach
Click a decision or use ↑ ↓
One primary action per section, in a visible order

I reworked the site's user flow and navigation so visitors could orient themselves quickly, find relevant information with less effort, and move toward a next step without backtracking.

Every page needed to answer three questions:

Where am I?
What matters here?
What should I do next?
Value first, in the visitor's words

I strengthened the messaging and content hierarchy to communicate what Stack Builders does, who it helps, and why its approach matters, before visitors had to search for the answer.

The goal was to turn the site into a clearer conversation: one that connects the company's capabilities to the visitor's needs from the first moments of the experience.

Proof placed where the decision happens

I elevated the visual design and interaction patterns to create a more modern, engaging experience that better reflected the quality of Stack Builders' work.

This was not decoration for its own sake. Every visual choice was designed to support comprehension, build trust, and keep visitors moving forward.

05 · What we expect

A front door still being built

The redesign is built to give Stack Builders a stronger digital front door, one that earns attention, makes the company's value easier to understand, and converts more interest into action.

By connecting clearer messaging, intuitive navigation, and a more expressive visual system, the new experience is built to turn more visits into exploration, and more exploration into meaningful engagement.

Since the site has not launched, these are the targets the team is building toward, set against current baselines and the behaviour we saw in testing. My job now is to keep each new section honest to what tested well.
